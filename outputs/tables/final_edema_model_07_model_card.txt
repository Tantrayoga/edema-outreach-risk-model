
FINAL MODEL SUMMARY

Project question:
Can early encounter patterns among edema patients identify those likely to follow a future high-risk trajectory?

Cohort:
- Patients with edema diagnoses R60.0 or R60.9.
- Main model excludes patients aged 90+.
- Patients aged 90+ are analyzed separately because their risk is strongly age-driven.

Prediction point:
- 60 days after first edema diagnosis.

Features used:
- Pre-edema encounter history.
- First 60-day edema-window encounter history.
- Diagnosis complexity.
- Department/provider complexity.
- ED, hospital, inpatient, outpatient, and observation utilization.
- Length-of-stay history.
- First and last early-window encounter context.

Features intentionally excluded:
- Age / PatientBirthYearBin.
- MyChartStatus.
- Race and ethnicity.
- Marital status.
- Smoking status.
- Social determinant screening counts.
- Future utilization variables.

Target:
- Future high-risk edema trajectory.
- Positive if the patient was eventually marked deceased and later had a long/complex future trajectory.

Key evaluation:
- ROC AUC: 0.7040
- Average Precision: 0.1460
- Baseline positive rate in test set: 4.98%

Best metric for outreach:
- Top-risk capture table.
- This asks: if SVH contacts the highest-risk 5%, 10%, 20%, or 30% of patients, how many future high-risk cases are captured?

Recommended framing:
This is not a death-prediction model. It is an outreach-prioritization prototype.
The goal is to identify edema patients under 90 whose early encounter patterns resemble patients who later developed long, complex, high-risk trajectories.

Clinical/business interpretation:
Edema can appear as a relatively quiet outpatient signal. By focusing on early utilization patterns rather than age alone, SVH could prioritize follow-up for patients who may be starting to deteriorate before obvious hospital escalation.
